It is February 21st, late in the afternoon, and the hallway outside has grown quiet after the final class bell. Inside the creativity lab, only a few of us remain. At my workbench, my hands are steady on a model excavator I have been practicing on after school, testing its mechanical arm until it moves smoothly. As I adjust the frame, my teacher walks over, watches for a moment, and says something that changes the direction of my life: “Imagine if all students came here to practice and create something different. The whole world would be filled with a creative generation capable of transforming our society into a new era of technology and innovation.”

Those words hit my heart deeply. Looking down at the work in front of me, a sudden realization washed over me: the future my teacher described would not build itself. That responsibility belonged to me. In that quiet lab, I saw myself clearly for the first time—not just as a student passing time after class, but as someone the world genuinely needed if I kept focusing on what I was trying to build right now. From that afternoon on, I decided to become a creative problem-solver, the kind of person the world can rely on for help at any moment.

Carrying that vision forward required surviving an academic environment that tested every limit I had. When I entered my boarding school, I found myself competing alongside the top students from across the country. Unlike regular high schools, our boarding school followed a sophisticated, enriched curriculum that demanded a completely different level of thinking. Being challenged so intensely taught me what a determined person can truly hold in his hands. Standing firmly against that pressure—and eventually outshining the difficulty of that environment—showed me that overcoming hardship requires daily time, patience, and relentless effort. By waking early, obeying my instructors, and respecting every rule and regulation of our campus, I discovered who I am: someone who views self-discipline and respect for others as the foundation of success.

Working closely with such dedicated students also taught me that a single hard problem can be approached in many different ways. Outside the classroom, our boarding school brought together ambitious peers from every corner of society. When I led our school’s cultural difference group and served as an Abba Gadaa leader—a student role rooted in a traditional democratic system of peacemaking and consensus—I experienced firsthand how vital it is to value every person equally. In every aspect of campus life, I made it my mission to treat every human being with the same dignity regardless of background or history—whether Black or White, Muslim or Christian, or from any ethnicity. Listening to my peers and helping them understand one another taught me that innovation means very little if we cannot live and work together in harmony.

Today, whenever I step into a lab or sit down to tackle a complex challenge, I still hear my teacher’s words from February 21st. Transforming the world into an era of innovation takes more than technical skill; it requires the discipline to endure a demanding environment and the humility to unite people of every background. I am still walking that path, and I know the weight of the responsibility I have chosen to carry. Beneath the rigorous routines and the lessons of my boarding school, I see a creator and a leader ready to answer when the world calls.
